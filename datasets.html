Datasets

One entry per dataset configured in config/ (the public ones; the app links here from its Design and model tab, to the entry whose heading is the dataset id in lowercase). Each entry records what the dataset is, how it is modelled, and what to know before trusting it. The formulas and numbers come from the database (dataset_design, variance_summary); the reasons come from the config comments and the findings in Methods. Per-timepoint subsets (for example ANEMONES_DAY1) have no design of their own: they use their parent’s, so they link to the parent’s entry.

Reading the models

Every dataset with a design: block gets the same two models (R/lib/design.R, R/targets/variance.R).

  • cell. One factor for group × timepoint (time alone, or group alone, when the dataset has only one of them). In the differential expression model the formula is ~0 + cell + ..., so each coefficient is the mean expression of one cell and every question is a difference of cell means. This handles a group sampled at fewer timepoints than the others without special cases.
  • Variance model (variancePartition, ML). Asks how much of each gene’s variance each term explains; it tests nothing. Categorical variables are random effects, numeric covariates are fixed and standardized. Between patients is the patient term plus person-level variables (group, sex, age); time is time plus group × time; residual is change within a person that is not shared by time, plus noise. A variable with few levels gives a noisy variance estimate that is often exactly 0: read a 0 as “not distinguishable here”, not “no effect”.
  • Differential expression model (dream, REML). For a time course: each later timepoint against the first, within each group, plus a joint test per group, with a patient random intercept so that samples from one person are not treated as independent. A covariate that is constant within a person (sex) is absorbed by that term; it is not cancelled exactly when people contribute to different cells. Cross-sectional datasets compare groups pairwise.
  • Technical covariates (vp_covariates, or a control-probe control_factor) go into the variance model only.

Shares quoted below are variance-weighted over the genes the factor methods see (about 8,000): between patients / time / residual.

ANEMONES

ANEMONES (GSE236713), array, 447 samples from 194 patients; subsets ANEMONES_DAY1, _DAY2, _DAY5, _DISCHARGE.

Cells (samples / patients) Day 1 Day 2 Day 5 Discharge
Sepsis 125 / 124 93 / 93 69 / 69 37 / 37
SIRS 39 / 38 31 / 31 15 / 15 8 / 8
Control 30 / 30 - - -

The design is ragged: controls exist only at Day 1. A disease * timepoint model would contain interaction terms for Control at later days that have no data; the cell-means model has none and fits the same means where data exist. Control gets no time contrasts (one timepoint).

  • Variance model: ~(1|patient_id) + (1|timepoint) + (1|disease) + (1|disease:timepoint) + (1|sex) + control_factor.
  • DE model: ~0 + cell + (1|patient_id) + control_factor.
  • control_factor is the first principal component of the array’s positive control probes (96 ERCC, 10 Agilent E1A spike-ins and other controls; 72% of their variation). The deposited values have no between-sample normalization, and the sample-mean shift follows these controls (rho 0.92), not leukocyte composition. Shifting each sample by a control offset over-corrected (the controls move about twice as much as genes; sex agreement fell from 0.99 to 0.94), so the PC enters as a covariate instead.
  • Result: 34% between patients, 1.7% time, 31% residual, plus a technical share (the control factor). Time effects are real for individual genes (7,134 genes at FDR < 5% for Sepsis, Discharge against Day 1) but small as a share of variance.
  • Quality: sex agreement 0.99; 4 subjects have discordant sex annotations and stay in.

CORTICUS

CORTICUS (GSE106878), array, 94 samples from 47 patients, each sampled at Pre and Post(24h); subsets CORTICUS_PRE, CORTICUS_POST24H. No group.

  • Variance model: patient, timepoint, sex (random), age (fixed). DE: ~0 + cell + (1|patient_id), one contrast (Post against Pre).
  • time_order is required because Post(24h) sorts before Pre. Treatment, responder and 28-day survival are left out of the models because they can mediate the time effect.
  • The array has no control probes; probes the annotation marks as matching no transcript stand in as a technical reference for the composition check.
  • Result: 56% between patients, 3.5% time, 40% residual. PC1 tracks the neutrophil-lymphocyte score (rho -0.60).

EARLI

EARLI, RNA-seq, 189 samples, cross-sectional (one per person). Groups: lca_label (Hyper- or Hypo-inflammatory subphenotype); covariates sex and age.

  • No subject or time, so there is no between-patient term: the variance model is group, sex, age and residual, and the DE model compares the two subphenotypes.
  • Result: 94% residual. The subphenotype carries about 5% of the variance.

GSE110487

GSE110487, RNA-seq, 62 samples from 31 patients at T1 and T2; subsets GSE110487_T1, GSE110487_T2. No group or covariates (the only other column, response to treatment, is left out).

  • Result: 43% between patients, 1.2% time, 56% residual. PC1 is strongly the neutrophil-lymphocyte axis (rho -0.83), and that score is 61% patient-level: leukocyte composition is largely a stable person trait here.

GSE13904

GSE13904, array, 227 samples from 186 patients at day1 and day3; 41 patients have both, 145 only one. Subsets GSE13904_DAY1, GSE13904_DAY3.

  • Group: clinical_status, taken at each subject’s first timepoint (group_at_first_time), because a patient’s status changes within person for 17 of the 41 sampled twice (for example Septic Shock to SIRS resolved).
  • No sex or age columns. Affymetrix hybridization spike-ins serve as the technical reference.
  • Result: 59% between patients, 1.8% time, 39% residual. 98% of the per-gene fits are singular (a variance component estimated at exactly 0), and 145 of the 186 patients have a single sample, so per-gene shares are noisier here than in datasets where everyone is sampled twice.

GSE273700

GSE273700, RNA-seq, 104 samples from 52 patients, each at Day1 and Day8; subsets GSE273700_DAY1, GSE273700_DAY8. No group or covariates.

  • Result: 39% between patients, 2% time, 59% residual. PC1 is the neutrophil-lymphocyte axis (|rho| 0.90).

GSE54514

GSE54514, array, 163 samples; timepoint day 1-5 (modelled as categorical), person id group_id (for example HC_1); group disease_status (healthy, sepsis survivor, sepsis nonsurvivor); covariates sex and age. Healthy controls are sampled at day 1 and day 5 only; patients across the range. Subsets GSE54514_DAY1 to _DAY5.

  • Data quality: this dataset’s sample annotations do not match its arrays. Sex inferred from chrY and XIST genes agrees with the recorded sex for only 50% of samples, and samples of one group_id disagree on sex in 35 of 54 subjects. It stays in the pipeline and the app, flagged, but its subject and time results are not interpretable as person-level results.
  • Result: 7% between patients, 10% time, 83% residual: the near-absence of a stable between-patient share is consistent with the “subjects” not being real individuals.

GSE95233

GSE95233, array, 102 samples from 51 patients at D01, D02, D03 (22 healthy-control samples with a missing timepoint are dropped). Covariates sex and age; the recorded sex H (homme) is recoded to M (all four samples show the male XIST/chrY pattern). Subsets GSE95233_D01, _D02, _D03.

  • Result: 38% between patients, 4.2% time (the largest time share among the well-formed designs), 58% residual. Sex agreement 0.98 (one patient discordant).

MARS

MARS (GSE65682), array, 802 samples, cross-sectional. Group healthy_control (42 healthy against 760 patients); covariates sex and age. There is no subject or time column (the title identifiers are not repeated measures). No between-patient term; 75% residual.

dilgom

dilgom (E-TABM-1036), array, 518 samples, a healthy reference cohort, cross-sectional. Group age_band (five levels); covariate sex; one DE contrast (65-74 against 25-34). 97% residual.

hfgp-500fg

hfgp-500fg (GSE134080), RNA-seq, 100 samples, a healthy reference cohort, cross-sectional. Group sex, covariate age. It is a positive control: the top sex-differential genes should be on chromosome Y. Sex agreement 1.0. Whether a healthy cohort belongs in a sepsis analysis is an open scope question recorded in its config.

SHIP-TREND

SHIP-TREND (GSE36382), array, 991 healthy samples. Its phenotype data is access restricted, so there is no sample metadata: it gets factor fits but no design, variance or composition analysis. It is the largest dataset (8,000 genes × 991 samples) and sizes the controllers’ memory.

gains

gains: four pooled ArrayExpress accessions (E-MTAB-4421, 4451, 5273, 5274), array, no timepoint. Not in the pipeline: its preprocessing script is a stub until it is decided whether to pool the four accessions.
