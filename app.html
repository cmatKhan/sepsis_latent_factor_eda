Launching the app

Note

The app is built on schema 1 of the redesigned database. Schema 2 (Database) adds the repeated-measures tables (dataset_design, samples, variance_*, factor_variance, …) and moves the enrichment gene lists (enrichment_results.genes) and the pattern_markers table into per-dataset artifacts; the app doesn’t read those yet. This page describes the app as it is.

Launching

Sys.setenv(STABILITY_DB = "results/stability.sqlite")   # optional
shiny::runApp("app")
STABILITY_DB=/path/to/stability.sqlite Rscript -e "shiny::runApp('app')"
  • STABILITY_DB picks the database; if unset, the app uses db_path from config/pipeline.yml (launched from the project root or app/).
  • The app opens the DB read-only. Datasets, methods and fits are queried live.

Layout

The sidebar holds the choices every page shares: dataset, method and rank (WGCNA: power; the rank defaults to the method’s optimal fit), and the similarity metric (cosine, Pearson, Spearman: which per-factor stability is read). A page has its own selector only when it needs a second dataset or fit (Projection comparison’s target and Fit B, the WGCNA overlap’s other power) or a choice inside the fit (factor, module, field). The Compare settings panel holds what only the Compare page uses: the methods to set side by side and the optimal fit rule.

The Compare page has one tab per question.

Tab Question Source
1. Optimal hyperparameter which rank (WGCNA: power) is best for each method on this dataset representative fits (v_fits), factors.stability_*, fit_metrics, fit_pairs.ari
2. Best method which method is best and most stable on this dataset the optimal fits, plus median projectR R² from projections
3. Across methods how similar are the optimal factors of different methods computed live from each optimal fit’s loadings (WGCNA: module membership) and scores
4. Across datasets how similar are a method’s factors across datasets projections (sample space) and gene_space_agreement (gene space)

Time structure

The first page (before Compare) shows how much of each time-course dataset’s variance is between subjects, an average time effect (the change every subject shares between timepoints), or other within-subject variation (change that differs between subjects, plus measurement noise; the two cannot be separated without technical replicates). It decides how much of what a factor can find is about change over time.

  • All time-course datasets: the split for each pooled dataset (ANEMONES, CORTICUS, GSE13904, GSE54514, GSE95233, GSE110487, GSE273700), with subjects, samples, subjects measured more than once and timepoints. The whisker is a 95% interval from resampling subjects (the time part is shown without an interval because its interval is unreliable near zero).
  • Across genes: the distribution of each gene’s between-subject share for the selected dataset’s family, once with every gene counted equally and once weighted by gene variance (which is what the pooled value reflects).
  • Factors of the selected fit (choose a pooled dataset in the sidebar): the same split of each factor’s sample scores, which separates factors that describe stable differences between people from factors with a within-subject component.

Method. A sample’s timepoint is the sibling dataset whose matrix contains it (config/dataset_families.yml); subjects come from each dataset’s subject_id_col. For each gene (or factor) the sums of squares are split sequentially into subject, then timepoint within subject, then the rest, and converted to variance components with the degrees of freedom each part uses: within = (SS_time + SS_resid)/(N - S); between = (SS_between/(S - 1) - within)/n0, where n0 is the average number of samples per subject; time = max(SS_time - (T - 1) x resid, 0)/N; resid = SS_resid/(N - S - (T - 1)). Raw sums-of-squares shares would overstate the between-subject part (with two samples per subject even pure noise puts about half of the sum of squares there). Pooled values are ratios of sums over genes, so high-variance genes count more. On simulated data with known components the estimates recover them (0.573 against a true 0.58), and with no subject effect the between-subject estimate is 0.004. The split is computed when the page is opened (about 25 s the first time) and cached for the session.

The pipeline now stores the model-based version of this split: per dataset and gene from variancePartition (variance_summary, variance_terms, variance_categories, the variance_partition artifact) and per factor (factor_variance); see Methods, “Repeated measures”. Switching this page to the stored values is pending.

Explore fits

A second page (top navigation) shows the sidebar’s method on the selected dataset, across its hyperparameter (One rank in detail uses the sidebar rank). Tabs depend on the method:

Method Tabs

NMF, CoGAPS stability vs rank; reconstruction error across seeds; one rank in detail

WGCNA module agreement (ARI) between powers; modules, unassigned genes and scale-free fit by power; for the sidebar power: module sizes and kME of members vs non-members vs the permutation null, the gene dendrogram with module colours, and module overlap with another power

The optimal fit

The DB stores one representative fit per rank but no chosen rank, so the app picks it:

  • Highest quality that is still stable (default): among ranks whose stability is at least the threshold (default 0.9), the one with the highest in-sample quality. If none qualifies, the most stable rank. Quality rises with rank, so this is the largest rank whose factors still reproduce across seeds.
  • Highest in-sample quality: ignores stability, so it usually picks the largest rank.

In-sample quality is 1 - mse / val_sd² for PCA, NMF, CoGAPS and ICA; sPCA stores no mse, so its sparse proportion of variance explained is used; WGCNA has no reconstruction, so its mean member kME is used. Quality is comparable within a method, not across methods. Stability is the mean over factors of the median matched similarity across same-rank fit pairs; PCA’s nested components are stable by construction (1), and WGCNA uses the mean ARI against the other powers.

Cross-method agreement matches factors one-to-one (Hungarian, on |Jaccard| of the top 50 genes per factor) and averages the matched overlaps, or clusters samples by each fit’s scores (k = 4) and compares partitions by ARI. Cross-dataset views are ordered by hierarchical clustering with the two directions of each pair averaged.

Inspecting one fit

The sidebar’s method and rank drive the Inspect fit pages, Trajectory across time, Explore fits and Projection comparison (Fit A). For WGCNA the rank is the soft-threshold power and a factor is a module: Factor detail adds Module quality (kME of members, non-members and the permutation null) and Module genes (with gene significance against kME) tabs, and Metadata shows module-trait relationships. Views with no WGCNA data (cross-dataset similarity, trajectory, projection, pattern drivers, gene biplot) say so instead of hiding the page. PCA at rank n is its single max-rank fit’s first n components.
