Methods

Six latent-factor methods are fit to every dataset. Each lives in one file, R/methods/<name>.R, discovered automatically by discover_method_registries() (R/lib/method_registry.R).

The methods and their parameters

A method runs for a dataset when it appears under the config’s methods: block (WGCNA nests under methods.network). Each key is the underlying tool’s own argument name. A scalar is a fixed setting; a list is a swept grid dimension, crossed with the others in the method’s build_grid(). A key only takes effect if that build_grid() uses it; see each method file for its keys and defaults.

Method Tool Config keys Stability design
pca prcomp() rank (only its maximum is used) deterministic
nmf NNLM::nnmf() k (swept), seed (swept; applied with set.seed()) across seeds
cogaps CoGAPS::CoGAPS() params (nPatterns, seed, nIterations, distributed), distributed_params, run across seeds
spca elasticnet::spca() K (swept), K_scan, para_scan, para_step, sparse, lambda (one value), max.iter, eps.conv, max_hours parameter grid
ica fastICA::fastICA() n.comp (swept), seed (swept), bootstrap (swept), alpha (one value) across seeds and resamples
network.wgcna WGCNA::blockwiseModules() power (swept), minModuleSize, mergeCutHeight, networkType, maxBlockSize parameter grid

PCA: one fit per dataset

PCA components are nested – the first k components of a rank-20 fit are the rank-k solution – so PCA is fit once per dataset, at the largest configured rank. The fit also records mse_by_rank, every truncation’s reconstruction error. Choosing the first n components is left to ingestion and the app.

sPCA: choosing the sparsity

sPCA runs with sparse = "varnum", under which para is the fraction of genes allowed a non-zero loading in each component, converted to a count from the dataset’s gene number. Fit time grows steeply with para and K: for EARLI (7465 genes × 189 samples) the median fit took 1.1 min at para 0.1 and K 2–5, but 158 min at para 0.5 and K 16–20.

How the sparsity is chosen. We follow Guerra-Urzola, Van Deun, Vera & Sijtsma (2021), “A Guide for Sparse PCA: Model Comparison and Applications”, Psychometrika 86(4):893–919 (PMC8636462): fix K, then choose the sparsity that maximizes the Index of Sparseness

IS = PEV_sparse × PEV_pca × PS
  • PEV_sparse: variance explained by the sparse components.
  • PEV_pca: variance explained by the first K ordinary principal components (from the dataset’s PCA fit).
  • PS: the proportion of zero loadings; under varnum, PS = 1 − para.

The authors report that IS beat cross-validation and BIC at recovering the true sparsity, with a single interior peak in their applications (97% sparsity for gene expression).

The search runs in two stages per dataset (spca_build_grid(), spca_refine_grid() in R/methods/spca.R):

  1. Scan: one K (K_scan, default 5) at para_scan = 0.2, 0.3, 0.4, 0.5.
  2. Refine: the scan’s IS winner p*, then every K (2–12) at p* − para_step, p* and p* + para_step (default 0.05), skipping pairs the scan already fit.

That’s about 36 fits per dataset. selection_spca_<dataset> (spca_select_fits()) tabulates IS for every fit and marks the best para per K; those fits are sPCA’s representative fits. If the scan’s winner sits at the edge of para_scan, the refinement still runs, the selection table records at_scan_edge and a warning suggests widening para_scan for that dataset. K_scan, para_scan and para_step can be set per dataset in methods: spca:.

  • Why one sparsity per dataset. On the earlier full grid (30 datasets, K 2–12, para 0.02–0.5; 2,968 fits, 1,451 CPU-hours) every IS winner was at para 0.25–0.50, and para ≤ 0.2 never won. The winner moved by at most 0.10 across K within a dataset but differed between datasets (GSE54514 0.25, GSE95233 0.35, ANEMONES 0.45). Replaying those fits with a scan at K = 5 and the winner ± 0.05 for every other K landed within 0.05 of each K’s winner at ≥ 80% of the Ks in all 30 datasets, losing a mean 0.004% of IS (worst 0.63%), for about half the compute. A lower scan K was noisier and a higher one costlier without agreeing better.
  • lambda is not swept. We tested λ ∈ {1e-6, 0.1, 1} under varnum on EARLI: across 113 (K, para) pairs fit at both 1e-6 and 1, total PEV differed by a median of 1.5e-4 and at most 5.7e-4. Under varnum, para fixes the number of non-zero loadings, leaving λ almost nothing to change (the paper makes the same point about LARS-EN). lambda is one value, default 1e-6, and spca_build_grid() rejects a list.
  • Convergence. spca() runs with trace = TRUE, which prints its iteration count every 10 iterations; run_spca_job() captures it and records iterations (a lower bound, to within 10) and whether max.iter was reached without converging (converged in fit_metrics; hit_max_iter in the selection table, where such fits stay, flagged).
  • The 8-hour stop. A fit still running after max_hours (default 8) stops itself and is recorded as failed (killed_not_converged), with the iterations it reached. On the earlier grid only two fits ended this way (ANEMONES para 0.05, K 10 and 11), in the low-para range the scan no longer covers.
  • Scores are computed on the centered matrix, matching how spca() fits the model.

ICA, ICASSO and kurtosis

ICA (fastICA, after a PCA prewhitening step; run_ica_seed_sweep_job() in R/methods/ica.R) is fit across seeds and, with bootstrap = TRUE, on bootstrap resamples. A resample draws subjects with replacement and takes all of each drawn subject’s samples, so a person’s repeated samples stay together; drawing single samples would leave most people in every replicate through their other samples and overstate stability. Subjects come from fit_subjects() (R/targets/run_method_fit.R, the subjects_<dataset> target); in a cross-sectional dataset every sample is its own subject, which is the ordinary bootstrap. A bootstrap fit keeps its sources under the resampled sample ids (boot_idx, boot_subjects) but has no per-sample scores.

ICASSO (Himberg, Hyvärinen & Esposito 2004, NeuroImage 22(3):1214–1222) pools every component of every fit at one rank – seeds and bootstrap resamples together – clusters them by |cosine| (average linkage, cut to the rank), and scores each cluster with Iq = mean within-cluster similarity − mean similarity to everything outside it. Iq ≥ 0.7 is the usual “trustworthy” threshold.

Per-component kurtosis (Lee & Batzoglou 2003) is the non-Gaussianity fastICA maximizes; a clearly positive excess kurtosis is the expected signature of a real biological source.

WGCNA: one network over all genes

For each soft-thresholding power, WGCNA correlates every pair of genes across samples and raises the correlations to that power, suppressing weak ones. It then computes the topological overlap (TOM) – how many strong partners two genes share – builds a gene tree from 1 − TOM, and cuts it into modules of at least minModuleSize genes. Modules whose eigengenes (first principal components) correlate above 1 − mergeCutHeight are merged.

These genes × genes matrices grow with the square of the gene count, so blockwiseModules() by default caps a network at 5,000 genes and splits larger data into blocks, building separate networks within each. Genes in different blocks are then never compared, and the block count depends on the gene count. We set maxBlockSize = 50000, so each fit is one network over all genes (about 7,500), as the WGCNA authors recommend whenever memory allows. Its gene tree is then the one the modules were cut from, and ingestion stores it directly. Workers request 6 GB for this.

Each fit (run_wgcna_param_job(), R/methods/wgcna.R) also stores every gene’s connectivity (total, within its module, outside it, and the difference; intramodularConnectivity.fromExpr()), the scale-free fit of that connectivity at the fit’s power (scaleFreeFitIndex()), and the goodSamplesGenes() result. The TOM itself (genes × genes per fit) is not saved; it can be rebuilt from the dataset’s matrix and the fit’s parameters.

Error handling and timings

Every fit function runs inside capture_fit() (R/lib/method_registry.R): a failing fit returns its error message instead of stopping its batch, and every result records its wall time. sPCA additionally stops itself at max_hours.

Representative fits

Downstream analyses (enrichment, projections, redundancy, pattern drivers) use each method’s representative fits (fits.representative in the DB):

Adding a method

Write one R/methods/<name>.R and add a controller named <name> to config/pipeline.yml. The file defines three things.

1. A fit function that reads its matrix from the free variable mat (or mat_nn, the non-negative shift) and, if it resamples, each column’s subject from subjects; run_method_fits() (R/targets/run_method_fit.R) binds both for each call. Its arguments are the columns your build_grid() produces. Wrap the body in capture_fit() so a failure is recorded rather than stopping the batch, and return as much of the model as is cheap to store: ingestion can then change without refitting.

2. <name>_registry, the fitting contract (the variable name must match the file name):

Field Meaning
global_object "mat" or "mat_nn"
fn the fit function
build_grid function(params) returning one row per fit, usually expand.grid() over the fit function’s arguments; params is defaults merged with the dataset config (merge_named_list())
defaults optional default parameters
pkgs optional packages to load on the worker
resource_defaults optional function(params, resources) for defaults from the worker’s resources (cpus_per_task)
needs_nonneg TRUE to fit on the non-negative shift
network TRUE if the config nests under methods.network
refine_grid + select_fits optional data-driven second stage (as sPCA’s search)

3. <name>_ingest, the ingestion contract (validate_method_ingest()):

Field Meaning
family "seed_sweep" or "param_grid"
sign_ambiguous TRUE if factors are unique only up to sign (matching uses |similarity|; enrichment tests both ends)
has_loadings FALSE for a method without a genes × factors matrix (WGCNA)
resource_params flattened parameter names that only set parallelism
extract(result, params) one fit’s result → fit_record_from(): rank, seed, mse, loadings, scores, diagnostics, the raw object, scalar metrics

Why two contracts. The fit targets depend on the registry and everything it calls, so editing them refits the method – for sPCA, about a day of compute. The ingestion contract is referenced only by ingestion targets, so changing what’s extracted never refits.

R/methods/pca.R is the simplest example; cogaps.R shows sub-blocks, wgcna.R a network method without loadings, spca.R a refine stage.

Methods considered and dropped

  • CP and Tucker tensor decompositions (genes × subjects × timepoints) were implemented and removed on 2026-09-29. The planned data has only 2 timepoints per subject, too few to fit a time mode or choose its rank; tensor rank selection is NP-hard and CP/Tucker results are very sensitive to hyperparameters (summary).
  • ICAclust (Nascimento et al., PLoS One 2017) clusters genes by temporal shape with ICA over timepoints; with 2 timepoints its rotation has a single free angle, so the method is degenerate. Never adopted.
  • Masking cross-validation and subsample projection stability designs were explored and removed; see git history to revive them.

Repeated measures: variance partition, differential expression and sample checks

Most datasets sample each patient more than once. Before deciding how the factor analysis should treat that, the pipeline measures, per gene, how expression variance splits into between-person, time and the rest (variancePartition), tests change over time (dream), and checks each dataset’s samples (leukocyte composition, technical scale, recorded vs inferred sex). It runs for every dataset with a design: block (see Data, “The design: block”) and never touches a factor fit, so it can be rebuilt on its own.

Model tasks run on the vp controller (BiocParallel MulticoreParam, forked workers over its CPUs) and record their own peak memory and CPU time from the job’s cgroup.

Models

The models use each platform’s own processing, not the factor-analysis matrix (which is filtered to the top-variance genes), as the variancePartition vignettes recommend.

  • Variance partition (ML, as the vignette recommends for fractions): ~ (1|subject) + (1|time) + (1|group) + (1|group:time) + (1|categorical covariates) + numeric covariates. Categorical variables are random effects; numeric covariates are standardized (a fraction, var(Xβ), doesn’t depend on scale; centring keeps a coefficient off the intercept, scaling keeps lme4 from refusing predictors on very different scales). The terms fall into categories: subject, subject-level (variables fixed within a person: sex, age, arm), time, group × time, technical (vp_covariates), covariate, residual. The between-subject total is subject + subject-level, as the vignette’s nesting note says; the residual fraction is reported but not used as a metric (the vignette’s FAQ).
  • dream (REML; Kenward-Roger for ≤ 20 samples, else Satterthwaite): ~ 0 + cell + (1|subject) plus any de_covariates, cell = group × time. Time courses test each timepoint against the first within each group with at least 3 repeatedly sampled subjects, plus a joint F-test per group; cross-sectional datasets test group pairs. z.std and F.std make statistics comparable across genes with different degrees of freedom.
  • Checks: dream against duplicateCorrelation() (dupcor_<dataset>; the genome-wide consensus matches the median subject fraction), canCorPairs() between design variables, per-gene collinearity scores and singular-fit flags, and the vignettes’ plots (violin and per-gene bars, expression of each term’s top gene, correlation structure, contrast matrix, dream vs duplicateCorrelation p-values, voom trend).

Sample checks

analyze_composition() (composition_<dataset>) records, per sample:

  • Leukocyte composition: neutrophil and lymphocyte marker scores (config/composition_markers.yml) and their difference, against each sample’s expression spread, location and leading principal components.
  • A technical reference: the array’s own control probes where the platform has them (ANEMONES’ Agilent spike-ins, the Affymetrix hybridization controls), a background stand-in for Illumina series matrices (probes matching no transcript), and library size and TMM factor for RNA-seq. The same sample-level traits also go through the dataset’s variance formula.
  • Sex: inferred from chrY genes against XIST and compared with the recorded sex; subjects whose samples disagree are counted. This catches sample annotations that don’t belong to their arrays.

Findings (2026-10-13): in the RNA-seq time courses PC1 is the neutrophil-lymphocyte axis (|rho| 0.83–0.90), not library size. ANEMONES’ sample-mean shift follows its spike-in controls (rho 0.92), not composition, so its control probes’ first principal component enters its models as a covariate (control_probes: {covariate: true}): shifting each sample by a control offset over-corrected, because the controls move about twice as much as genes. GSE54514’s sample annotations don’t match its arrays (sex agreement 0.50; samples of one group_id disagree on sex in 35 of 54 subjects), so its subject and time results aren’t interpretable. Smaller sex discordances: ANEMONES (4 subjects), GSE95233 (Patient 198), GSE13904 and GSE273700 (1 subject each), EARLI (3 samples).

What the variance partition says about repeated measures

From variance_explore (2026-10-13; the numbers are in the DB’s variance_summary):

  • Time is a small part of the signal factorization sees. In the top 8,000 genes, time plus group × time is 1–4% of variance; between-subject is 35–60%; most of the remainder is within-person variation. Factors fit on a full time-course dataset are therefore mostly person-level structure, the same structure a single timepoint contains (median single-timepoint repeatability 0.25–0.5).
  • The main biological axis is a stable person trait. Leukocyte composition, which drives PC1 in the RNA-seq data, is 40–70% subject and about 0–7% time.
  • There is little within-person dynamics to recover. Change between a person’s samples shares no dominant direction (ROSE: its first principal component is 9% of the change) and isn’t explained by composition change; removing each person’s mean would leave factors fit mostly to noise. ROSE’s high residual share comes from noisy low-expressed genes (its subject share rises from 0.08 to 0.34 across expression quartiles).

So the factor analysis fits the full datasets and every per-timepoint subset as they are, and handles repeated measures in how stability is measured and read:

  • Resampling keeps a person’s samples together (ICA’s bootstrap, above).
  • Within-family comparisons are not replication. A full dataset and its timepoint subsets, or two subsets, share people: their agreement measures temporal reproducibility within a cohort. Only comparisons between cohorts test replication.
  • Every factor is described by the same model (factor_vp_<method>_<dataset>, factor_variance_partition()): its sample scores go through the dataset’s variance formula, giving each factor’s share of between-person, time, group, technical and residual variance. A per-timepoint subset uses its parent’s design restricted to its samples, without subject and time terms.
